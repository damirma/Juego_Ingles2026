REQUIRED EVIDENCE — GRAMMAR JEOPARDY
=====================================

1. NAME OF THE GAME
--------------------
Grammar Jeopardy: Used To & Passive Voice Challenge

2. GAME OBJECTIVE
------------------
Practice and demonstrate the correct use of "used to" and the passive voice
(present and past) through a competitive, Jeopardy-style trivia game played
in teams.

3. INSTRUCTIONS / RULES
-------------------------
1. Split the class into 2 to 4 teams.
2. Teams take turns choosing a category and a point value (100-500) from
   the board.
3. Read the question out loud. The team has a short time to answer.
4. Press "Show Answer" to reveal the correct sentence.
5. Award the points to the team that answered correctly.
6. The question then disappears from the board.
7. The game ends when all 20 questions have been played. The team with
   the most points wins.

4. LIST OF GRAMMAR QUESTIONS / CHALLENGES
-------------------------------------------

CATEGORY: USED TO

  100 — Complete the sentence with the correct form of 'play': When I was
        a child, I ______ in the park every day.
  200 — Which sentence is grammatically correct?
        A) She use to live in Bogota.
        B) She used to live in Bogota.
        C) She uses to live in Bogota.
  300 — Rewrite in the negative form: "He used to smoke."
  400 — Find and correct the mistake: "I use to wake up early when I was
        in school."
  500 — Turn this into a yes/no question: "They used to live in Medellin."

CATEGORY: PASSIVE — PRESENT

  100 — Change to passive voice: "The teacher checks the homework every
        day."
  200 — Complete with the correct passive form: "The reports ______
        (send) every Monday."
  300 — Change to passive voice (don't mention who does it): "They
        deliver the packages on Fridays."
  400 — Find and correct the mistake: "The document is checking by the
        manager."
  500 — Change to passive voice: "Somebody cleans this office every
        morning."

CATEGORY: PASSIVE — PAST

  100 — Change to passive voice: "The company built this office in 2010."
  200 — Complete with the correct passive form: "The email ______ (send)
        yesterday."
  300 — Find and correct the mistake: "The cake were baked by my mom."
  400 — Change to passive voice: "Someone stole my phone last night."
  500 — Change to passive voice: "The workers finished the project two
        weeks ago."

CATEGORY: MIXED CHALLENGE

  100 — Choose the correct sentence:
        A) I used to be work here.
        B) I used to work here.
  200 — Is this sentence 'used to' (habit) or passive voice? "This app is
        used by millions of people."
  300 — Complete using 'used to': "In the 1990s, people ______ (not /
        have) internet at home."
  400 — Find and correct BOTH mistakes: "The bridge is build in 1930 and
        people use to cross it by foot."
  500 — FINAL CHALLENGE: Write one sentence using 'used to' AND one
        sentence in the passive voice, both about how technology has
        changed.

7. GRAMMAR JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------
This game practices "used to" and the passive voice by requiring players
to actively produce and correct both structures rather than just
recognize them. The "Used To" category covers the affirmative form
(used to + base verb), the negative form (didn't use to), and the
question form (Did ... use to...?), which are the three forms students
must master. The "Passive — Present" and "Passive — Past" categories
require transforming active sentences into passive ones (is/are + past
participle, was/were + past participle) and correcting common mistakes,
such as wrong auxiliary verbs or forgetting the past participle form.
The "Mixed Challenge" category forces players to tell "used to" and
passive voice apart in context, and the final question requires
combining both structures correctly in original sentences about
technology. Because every question requires either producing a full
correct sentence or spotting and fixing an error, the game goes beyond
memorization and tests real understanding of both grammar topics.
