REQUIRED EVIDENCE — GRAMMAR DETECTIVE AGENCY
=============================================

1. NAME OF THE GAME
--------------------
Grammar Detective Agency: The Case of the Missing Debate Trophy

2. GAME OBJECTIVE
------------------
Practice and demonstrate the correct use of "used to" and the passive voice
(present and past) by solving a mystery through deduction. Each of the 4
suspects has exactly 2 grammar clues tied to them. Answering both of a
suspect's clues correctly reveals whether that suspect is CLEARED (has an
alibi) or stays SUSPICIOUS. Only one suspect remains suspicious after all
8 clues — players must identify that suspect to close the case.

3. INSTRUCTIONS / RULES
-------------------------
1. Split the class into 2 to 4 detective teams.
2. Read the case introduction and the list of suspects out loud.
3. Read each grammar clue out loud, one at a time, in order (8 clues total,
   2 per suspect).
4. Teams try to answer using the correct grammar. Press "Reveal Answer" to
   check.
5. Award the point to the team that answered correctly (or press "No one
   got it" — the clue unlocks either way, so the case keeps moving and no
   team is stuck).
6. Press "Next Clue" to continue. Once BOTH of a suspect's clues are
   unlocked, that suspect's badge in the Suspects panel updates to
   ✅ Cleared or ⚠️ Suspicious.
7. When all 8 clues are unlocked, only one suspect should still be
   ⚠️ Suspicious. Click that suspect's card in the final screen to accuse
   them and close the case.
8. The team that solves the case correctly wins 300 bonus points (the
   teacher picks the team in the final screen), but everyone
   is invited to guess.

4. LIST OF GRAMMAR QUESTIONS / CHALLENGES
-------------------------------------------

CLUE 1 — Mr. Cruz — USED TO (affirmative)
  Complete with 'used to': Every night, Mr. Cruz ______ (lock) the front
  gate at exactly 9:00 PM, and the security camera confirms he did
  exactly that again this time.

CLUE 2 — Ms. Reyes — PASSIVE VOICE, PRESENT
  Change to passive voice: "The school keeps every master key inside a
  fingerprint-locked box in Ms. Reyes' office."

CLUE 3 — Tom — MIXED CHALLENGE
  Choose the correct sentence:
  A) Tom use to borrow books every Friday.
  B) Tom used to borrow books every Friday.

CLUE 4 — Diana — USED TO (negative)
  Rewrite in the negative form: "Diana used to leave right after her own
  team's practice."

CLUE 5 — Mr. Cruz — PASSIVE VOICE, PRESENT
  Complete with the correct passive form: "Every guard's location ______
  (record) automatically by the badge scanner at the front gate."

CLUE 6 — Ms. Reyes — PASSIVE VOICE, PAST
  Find and correct the mistake: "The key box were not opened at all that
  night, according to the digital log."

CLUE 7 — Tom — PASSIVE VOICE, PAST
  Complete with the correct passive form: "Tom ______ (not / see) in the
  library for the last two weeks, according to the sign-in sheet."

CLUE 8 — Diana — MIXED CHALLENGE, FINAL CLUE
  Complete using BOTH a 'used to' structure and a passive voice
  structure: "Most visiting teams ______ (not / know) about the trophy's
  new spot, because it ______ (move) to the library only last week — but
  Diana had just toured the building."

FINAL QUESTION — Who did it?
  Choose the culprit from the four suspects: Mr. Cruz (security guard),
  Ms. Reyes (school secretary), Tom (new exchange student), Diana (rival
  team captain).

5. ANSWER KEY
--------------

CLUE 1: used to lock
CLUE 2: Every master key is kept inside a fingerprint-locked box in
        Ms. Reyes' office.
CLUE 3: B — Tom used to borrow books every Friday.
CLUE 4: Diana didn't use to leave right after her own team's practice.
CLUE 5: is recorded
CLUE 6: The key box was not opened at all that night, according to the
        digital log.
CLUE 7: was not seen
CLUE 8: didn't use to know / was moved

SUSPECT VERDICTS (revealed automatically once both of a suspect's clues
are unlocked):
  Mr. Cruz  -> CLEARED  (Clues 1 + 5: his location is confirmed all
              night by the badge scanner).
  Ms. Reyes -> CLEARED  (Clues 2 + 6: the only keys that open the case
              never left their locked box that night).
  Tom       -> CLEARED  (Clues 3 + 7: he hadn't set foot in the library
              for two weeks).
  Diana     -> SUSPICIOUS (Clues 4 + 8: she broke her own routine that
              night, and she is the only visiting student who could have
              known the trophy's new location).

FINAL ANSWER: Diana. She is the only suspect who stays SUSPICIOUS once
all 8 clues are revealed — everyone else has a grammar-confirmed alibi.

7. GRAMMAR JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------
This game practices "used to" and the passive voice by requiring players
to actively produce and correct both structures in order to clear or
incriminate a suspect, rather than simply recognizing them. Clues 1 and 4
cover the affirmative and negative forms of "used to" (used to + base
verb, didn't use to + base verb), which describe past habits that
contrast with a break in routine — exactly the reasoning the mystery
depends on (Mr. Cruz's routine held up, Diana's routine broke). Clues 2,
5, 6 and 7 require transforming active sentences into passive ones
(is/are + past participle for the present, was/were + past participle
for the past) and correcting a common subject-verb agreement mistake in
the passive ("were" vs "was"). Clue 3 asks players to distinguish the
correct "used to" form from a common learner error ("use to" vs "used
to"), and Clue 8, the final clue, requires combining both a "used to"
structure and a passive voice structure in a single sentence, mirroring
how these forms are actually used together in real narration. Because
every clue requires producing a full correct sentence or spotting and
fixing an error before a suspect's status can be resolved, and because
the final accusation only makes sense if all 8 clues were understood
correctly, the game goes beyond memorization and tests real
understanding of both grammar topics through genuine deduction.
